Gamebook stories

Choose-your-own-adventure stories for the Gamebook app. Download the story pack, or turn a story you wrote in Twine or Ink into a .story file.

The story pack

Original stories, free to read and share (CC BY 4.0): five in English (a train mystery, a snowbound hut, a clock puzzle, a polar station, a fairy-tale market) and three in Turkish. Download the pack (zip)

Unzip it. Its EN and TR folders hold the stories in each language: copy the .story files you want (not the folder) into AppFiles/gamebook/ on the SD card.

Convert a story

  1. Add the story file:

    • Twine 2: Build → Publish to File gives an .html file; a Twee file (.twee, .tw) works too. Harlowe and SugarCube stories keep their variables and conditions; Chapbook and Snowman stories keep their text and links.
    • Ink: the .ink source file you write in Inky (not the compiled .json).
    • A .story file you wrote by hand: it is checked for broken links.
    Drop a story file here, or click to choose one .html, .twee, .tw, .ink, .story

  2. Check the result and download it.

      Show the .story text

    • Copy the .story file into AppFiles/gamebook/ on the reader's SD card (USB mode). It is in the app's list the next time you open Gamebook.

    What converts

    Passages, links and choices, variables (numbers, true / false, text) with (set:) / <<set>> / ~, conditions ((if:), (else-if:), (else:), <<if>>, Ink's {…}), printed variables, (link-goto:), (goto:), links that set variables, random numbers; Ink's knots, stitches, once-only and sticky choices, gathers and nested choices, diverts, glue and sequences.

    Left out, with a warning naming the passage: pictures and sound, styling, Harlowe's (display:), (click:) and other interactive hooks, SugarCube widgets and JavaScript, Ink tunnels, threads, lists and functions. The rest of the story still plays.

    Writing a story by hand? The format is plain text: the .story format.
