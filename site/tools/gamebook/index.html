Gamebook stories

Choose-your-own-adventure stories for the Gamebook app. Download the story pack, or turn a story made with Twine or Ink, or a Project Aon gamebook, into a .story file: yours, or one of the many shared online (see where to find stories).

The story pack

Original stories, free to read and share (CC BY 4.0): five in English (a train mystery, a snowbound hut, a clock puzzle, a polar station, a fairy-tale market) and three in Turkish. Download the pack (zip)

Unzip it. Its EN and TR folders hold the stories in each language: copy the .story files you want (not the folder) into AppFiles/gamebook/ on the SD card.

Convert a story

  1. Add the story file:

    • Twine 2: Build → Publish to File gives an .html file; a Twee file (.twee, .tw) works too. Harlowe and SugarCube stories keep their variables and conditions; Chapbook and Snowman stories keep their text and links.
    • Ink: the .ink source file you write in Inky (not the compiled .json).
    • Project Aon gamebooks (Lone Wolf, Grey Star, Freeway Warrior): the book's .epub from its page on projectaon.org.
    • A .zip holding one of these, or a Twine project's .twee files.
    • A .story file you wrote by hand: it is checked for broken links.

    Where to find stories

    Stories others have shared, ready for the converter. Each comes with its own licence: read it before sharing a converted file with anyone.

    • The IF Archive's Twine sources: the published .html files and Twee sources of Twine games, a few with open licences (Isolda's Eyes is CC BY 4.0, One King to Loot them All CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). The Twine games section has many more as playable .html.
    • IFDB, the Interactive Fiction Database: thousands of Twine and Ink games with reviews; many link a downloadable .html.
    • The Intercept by inkle, the makers of Ink (MIT licence): a wartime interrogation with many endings; its story is Assets/Ink/TheIntercept.ink.
    • Project Aon: Joe Dever's Lone Wolf series and more, free to download for your own use. On a book's page take the EPUB. The converted book opens on its rules; keep your Action Chart on paper as in the book, and the Combat Results Table from the book's PDF at hand. The Random Number Table is built in: the page shows a number whenever the book asks you to pick one. Project Aon's licence does not allow sharing the files, so convert your own copy.

    What converts well: stories of text, links, variables and conditions. Games built on scripts (RPG combat systems, inventories written in JavaScript, timers) keep their text and links but lose those parts, and the converter lists what it left out.

    What converts

    Passages, links and choices, variables (numbers, true / false, text) with (set:) / <<set>> / ~, conditions ((if:), (else-if:), (else:), <<if>>, Ink's {…}), printed variables, (link-goto:), (goto:), links that set variables, random numbers; Ink's knots, stitches, once-only and sticky choices, gathers and nested choices, diverts, glue and sequences.

    Also: (display:) and <<include>> (the passage's text in place), <<switch>>, back links (<<back>>, (link-undo:)), links to web pages (as text with the address), Ink functions that only change variables.

    Left out, with a warning naming the passage: pictures and sound, styling, timers, (click:) and other interactive hooks (their text is shown at once), SugarCube widgets and JavaScript, Ink tunnels, threads and lists. The rest of the story still plays.
